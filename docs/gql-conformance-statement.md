# zu's GQL conformance statement

Generated by `zu conformance --statement docs/conformance/zu.json`. Do not edit by hand: a test regenerates it and fails on drift.

ISO/IEC 39075:2024 Clause 24 says what a conformance claim has to contain and stops there. It defines no test suite, names no body that could adjudicate a claim, and there is no GQL certification programme anywhere to hold one to. Every GQL conformance statement in existence is therefore its author's own word, and this one would be too, so it is written the other way round: nothing on this page was typed by a person deciding what zu supports. Every number here is a count out of one measured run and every feature code here is on the page because a case citing that feature passed in that run. The run is named below and the command that reproduces it is at the bottom.

## Minimum conformance, Clause 24.2

Minimum conformance is all or nothing. An implementation either provides the mandatory language or it is not GQL, and no percentage means anything at this level, so the only useful thing a statement can say here is which of the two it is and what the evidence is.

**zu claims minimum conformance.** All 70 cases the corpus writes for the mandatory surface pass, none is skipped, and none failed to reach a verdict. What that is evidence of and what it is not is in the last section: the corpus is one reading of the standard's prose and nobody has adjudicated it.

## Conditions

The standard defines 68 GQLSTATUS conditions and zu answers a case with the right one for 61 of them. The rest are conditions no statement can reach here, and each one is named with its reason in `conformance.toml` at the root of this repository, because a condition that never fires because the engine cannot fail that way and a condition that never fires because the engine gets it wrong read identically from outside.

Across the whole run zu produced 47 distinct status codes, counted off the replies rather than off a summary, since a code the engine emitted once is the evidence that the machinery behind it works at all.

## What the standard leaves open, Clause 24.5.2

The other half of what Clause 24 asks for is not what zu supports but what zu chose where the standard declined to choose. That is 117 implementation-defined items and 20 implementation-dependent ones, and zu's answer to every one of them is in `docs/gql-implementation-defined.md`, generated by `zu conformance --implementation-defined`. Two of the answers are absences, stated as absences, and they are the reason two of the unreachable conditions above are unreachable.

## Extensions and options, Clause 24.5.3

zu adds no syntax to GQL. Every statement it accepts is a statement of the standard's grammar, and a statement outside that grammar is refused with a syntax error rather than given a meaning of zu's own, which is what makes the grammar cases above worth anything. There is no vendor prefix, no pragma and no dialect switch, so there is nothing here for a portable query to avoid.

What zu adds is outside the language: a command line, a JSON line protocol on a session, an Arrow encoding of a result, a loader that reads a SQLite database in zu's schema, and the conformance reporting this page is part of. None of those is reachable from a GQL-program and none changes what one means.

## How far the evidence reaches

The corpus behind this claim cites the standard mechanically, and the two numbers that say how much of the standard it has reached at all are these: 160 of the 317 normative subclauses have a passing case, and 403 of the 814 productions of the published grammar do. Those are properties of the corpus and not of zu. A subclause with no case is one nobody has written a case for; it is not one zu failed, and it is not one zu passed either.

## What this statement is not

It is not a certificate. ISO publishes no conformance test for GQL, recognises none published by anybody else, and Clause 24 does not contemplate one, so nobody is in a position to hand an engine a pass. The corpus behind these numbers is one careful reading of the standard's prose into executable cases, every case citing the subclause it came from, and a second competent reader could write a case that cites the same subclause and disagrees with it. There is no mechanism anywhere for settling that.

It is also a claim about one build on one machine on one day. Both are printed above for that reason.

## Reproducing it

The full report behind the tally is a megabyte of per-case timings, host readings and a wall clock, none of it the same twice, so it is not checked in anywhere. What is checked in is the tally this page is rendered from, and the four commands that regenerate the whole chain from an engine binary:

```
cargo build --release -p zu-cli
gql-compat run -adapter zu -binary target/release/zu -fail-on none -out reports/zu
zu conformance --tally reports/zu/zu.json > docs/conformance/zu.json
zu conformance --statement docs/conformance/zu.json > docs/gql-conformance-statement.md
```

The harness is [gql-compat](https://github.com/tamnd/gql-compat), which measures other engines the same way. `docs/gql-conformance.md` is the same corpus against zu, Neo4j and Ladybug side by side.
